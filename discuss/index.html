§ 01 — The idea in one minute

Let the machine tell you it's getting sick, before it stops.

01 — Watch

Small sensors

Clip-on sensors on critical parts: motors, gearboxes, bearings, blowers. They measure vibration, temperature and current.

02 — Learn

A box that learns "normal"

A small on-site unit learns how each machine behaves when it's healthy, taking speed, job and material into account.

03 — Warn

Early, plain warnings

When something starts drifting, maintenance gets a simple alert days or weeks ahead, with time to plan instead of react.

What it is not
  • A replacement for your maintenance team's experience
  • A new ERP, MES or plant-wide software project
  • Something that touches machine controls
  • Something I'm asking you to buy today

My own doubt

I suspect many of your stops are process issues like web breaks, register drift, ink problems and cylinder changeovers, not parts failing. If most lost hours come from process rather than mechanical failure, this idea covers only a small part of the problem. That's one of the main things I'd like your view on.

§ 05 — Where I'm unsure myself

The risks I already see.

01

False alarms

A few wrong alerts could make people stop trusting the system, which may be worse than having no system at all.

02

Others already do this

Large automation companies and some startups already sell vibration monitoring. What would make a small, focused option worth choosing?

03

Old lines vs. new lines

Newer machines may already have sensors built in. Older lines need retrofitting. I don't know which case is more common in your plants.

04

Who owns the problem

It could be the owner, the plant head or the maintenance head. The person who feels the pain may not be the one who signs off.
